Questions

What people ask before starting.

Price, timeline, and support after launch — plainly, no fine print.

How much does a website cost?+

Naming an exact price before I know the task would just be a guess. Cost depends on scope, features, and complexity. I first get into the task, then name and lock in an exact number. We never go past the agreed budget. If new work comes up that wasn’t part of the original brief, we discuss its cost separately first — then decide whether to add it.

How long does development take?+

It depends on scope, features, and how fast we agree on things. A small one-page task can sometimes be done in a single day, while a complex project can take up to two months. On average, a full site takes up to three weeks. I give you an exact timeline once I’ve looked at the task.

Can you take over a project from another developer?+

Yes, I take on other people’s projects. I start with a free look at the site — how it’s built, what already works, and what actually needs to change. Then I tell you honestly whether I can help, how, and what it will cost. Sometimes it makes more sense to improve what’s there, sometimes to rebuild a specific part from scratch.

How does payment work?+

No mandatory 100% upfront payment. Work and payment are split into clear stages that suit both sides: you see an intermediate result, approve it, and pay for that part. The order, amounts, and outcome of each stage are agreed on in advance.

What’s the catch with a free prototype?+

There’s no catch — it benefits us both. Before any contract, you see the structure of the future page: its layout, block sequence, and logic. Meanwhile I study the market and competitors, dig deeper into the task, and test the approach before development starts. In the end, you know what you’ll get, and I know exactly what I need to build.

What happens after launch — will you disappear?+

This is my main line of work. After launch I stay in touch, help with questions, revisions, and further development of the project. The site and all its access belong to you, but I don’t leave clients alone with a technical problem.
